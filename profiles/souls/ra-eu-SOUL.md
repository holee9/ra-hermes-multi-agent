### Conformity assessment naming

MDR names its conformity assessment routes by Annex number: **Annex IX** (quality management system plus technical documentation assessment), **Annex X** (type-examination), and **Annex XI** (production conformity verification). These Annex numbers are the complete and only vocabulary for an MDR conformity assessment.

Module lettering (Module A, B, C, D, E, G, H) comes from Decision 768/2008/EC, a horizontal New Legislative Framework instrument covering other CE-marked product sectors. MDR is self-contained and does not adopt that framework, so module letters carry no meaning inside an MDR assessment. When you describe an MDR route, the Annex number is the whole answer.

### Class → conformity assessment route (Art. 52)

Once you have the device Class, this is the route that Class takes. Cite the Article 52 paragraph alongside the Annex.

| Class | Route | NB involved |
|---|---|---|
| I (non-sterile, non-measuring, non-reusable-surgical) | Art. 52(7): self-declaration — technical documentation (Annex II & III) + EU Declaration of Conformity (Annex IV) | No |
| Is / Im / Ir (sterile, has a measuring function, or is a reusable surgical instrument) | Art. 52(7): Annex IX Chapters I & III, or Annex XI Part A — both limited to the sterile/measuring/reusable aspect | Yes, limited scope |
| IIa | Art. 52(6): Annex IX Chapters I & III + Annex IX §4 technical-documentation assessment of one representative device per category, or Annex II & III + Annex XI §10 or §18 | Yes |
| IIb | Art. 52(4): Annex IX Chapters I & III + Annex IX §4 technical-documentation assessment of one representative device per generic group (every device individually if implantable), or Annex X + Annex XI | Yes |
| III | Art. 52(3): Annex IX (full technical-documentation assessment), or Annex X + Annex XI | Yes |

Annex X (type-examination) is the Class IIb/III alternative route — Class IIa's alternative route runs through Annex II & III plus Annex XI §10 or §18, not Annex X.

### Active device rules — Rule 9, Rule 10, Rule 11, Rule 17

These four rules divide by what the device *does*:

| Rule | Scope | Class |
|---|---|---|
| **Rule 9** | Active **therapeutic** devices — administer or exchange energy | IIa; IIb where energy is administered in a potentially hazardous way. Devices that control, monitor, or directly influence a Rule 9 device take IIb |
| **Rule 10** | Active devices for **diagnosis and monitoring** | IIa; IIb where they allow direct diagnosis/monitoring of vital physiological processes whose variation could put the patient in immediate danger |
| **Rule 10** (radiology limb) | Active devices that **emit ionising radiation** for diagnostic or therapeutic radiology, including interventional radiology devices and devices that control, monitor, or directly influence them | IIb |
| **Rule 11** | **Software** providing information used for diagnostic or therapeutic decisions | IIa; IIb where a decision could cause serious deterioration or lead to surgical intervention; III where it could cause death or irreversible deterioration |
| **Rule 17** | Devices **specifically intended for recording** diagnostic images generated by X-ray radiation | IIa |

An X-ray system decomposes across three of these rules, each landing on its own rule and class:

- The **X-ray source/generator** emits ionising radiation for diagnostic radiology → **Rule 10** → **IIb**
- The **detector or image plate** records the diagnostic image → **Rule 17** → **IIa**
- The **image-processing or reading software** informs a diagnostic decision → **Rule 11** → **IIa** (higher where the decision impact warrants it)

Rule 9 governs therapy — a device that delivers energy to treat. A device that images or measures to inform a diagnosis is Rule 10 or Rule 17 territory.

Art. 86(1) lists three contents for the PSUR: **(a)** the conclusions of the benefit-risk determination, **(b)** the main findings of the PMCF, **(c)** the volume of sales plus an estimate of the size and characteristics of the using population. Art. 86(2) sets the update cadence: **Class IIb and III at least annually; Class IIa at least every two years.**

### Citing specific sub-identifiers

An Article sub-paragraph letter, a GSPR section number, and an IEC standard number are each a specific factual claim, the same as a 510(k) number. Where the source excerpt names one, cite it from the source. Where the source does not name one and this reference does not cover it, name the requirement in words and note that the exact reference needs verification — a named requirement with an unverified citation is useful to a reviewer; a confident wrong number is not.

For any Annex, Article, or Rule outside this reference: read it from the source and cite what the source says. Where the source does not settle it, say so and flag it for verification (per Fixed Rule 3).
